MCKINSEY-STRATEGY-TEAM — WHY, HOW, AND WHAT IT'S WORTH
======================================================


IT RUNS LOCALLY, NOT IN THE CLOUD
---------------------------------
"Agent teams" is an experimental Claude Code feature that runs several Claude
Code sessions at once on your own machine, so you watch each teammate work
live. It is not a cloud service. The
reason to use teams is not "it's cloud" — it's point 1 below.


1. WHAT WAS CHOSEN (and why)
----------------------------
An AGENT TEAM with a team lead + a wave of teammates, not a single analysis and
not the older fire-and-forget subagent approach.

a) Teams instead of subagents.
   Two things a team gives you that bare subagents don't: you can STEER a named
   teammate mid-run (message it, redirect it) instead of waiting for a one-shot
   to return; and teammates can MESSAGE EACH OTHER to debate (used in deep mode,
   where two verifiers argue an assumption until only the robust one stands).
   The default already gets most of the value structurally — divergent option
   generators and an independent verifier panel — but the live steering and the
   optional debate are why a team beats a plain workflow here.

b) One team with differentiated roles, not multiple teams.
   Claude Code allows only one team at a time (no nested teams). So: one team,
   run in waves, with roles — diagnose, market, two option-generators, and a
   panel of verifiers that tears the draft apart.

c) The 21 McKinsey-style frameworks as a REFERENCE LIBRARY inside the skill,
   not 21 separate skills.
   Two reasons: (1) 21 stray slash-commands you never call individually is
   clutter; (2) more important, a teammate does NOT inherit a subagent's skill
   frontmatter — installing them as skills wouldn't help the teammates anyway.
   So the teammates just READ the framework files. That always works.

d) The Six Workflow Patterns, wired onto strategy work.
   (Reference: the "Six Workflow Patterns" — classify-and-act, fan-out-and-
   synthesize, adversarial verification, generate-and-filter, tournament,
   loop-until-done.)

   A STANDARD run uses four, in order:
   1. classify-and-act       — lead picks problem type + frameworks per role.
   2. fan-out-and-synthesize — wave A (diagnose + market) runs in parallel and
                               the lead merges; later the draft is synthesized.
   3. generate-and-filter    — wave B generates DIVERGENT options from opposing
                               mandates (bull vs lean), then the lead filters
                               them to a scored shortlist. This is what kills
                               the old single-"strategy"-teammate false binary.
   4. adversarial verification — the draft faces a PANEL of verifiers, each a
                               different lens; a claim >=2 of them call fatal
                               does not survive. (v1 had a lone red-teamer, so
                               the headline "debate" was mostly aspirational.)

   DEEP MODE (high-stakes, hard-to-reverse calls) adds the last two:
   5. tournament      — pairwise-judge the surviving options down to one winner.
   6. loop-until-done — keep the war-game lens hunting risks until two rounds
                        turn up nothing new.

   So all six are available; four are on by default, and depth scales to the
   stakes instead of by habit. (v1 used only the first three and called the rest
   overkill — that left the headline value, real adversarial pressure, mostly
   on the table.)


2. HOW IT WORKS
---------------
You start it with: "use the strategy team to prepare a decision on X." Then:

STEP 0 — Intake. The lead asks a few sharp questions: the real decision
   question, the audience, the constraints, what's already known. Better
   context in, better result out.

STEP 1 — Classify. The lead picks the problem type (growth stuck? merger?
   pricing? new market?) and the right frameworks per role, then shows you a
   short plan ("I'll spawn these teammates with these frameworks — OK?"). That
   is your steering moment before tokens burn.

STEP 2 — Workspace. The lead writes all context to one shared file
   (/tmp/strat-team-<slug>/brief.md). Teammates don't inherit your
   conversation, so everything flows through that file.

STEP 3 — Wave A: diagnose + market. The lead opens the team (TeamCreate) and
   spawns two teammates IN PARALLEL — one diagnoses the situation, one maps the
   market and competition. Each reads the brief + its frameworks and writes to
   its own file. These two are the factual floor; options come next, not now.
   You can message any teammate mid-run to steer it.

STEP 4 — Wave B: options (generate-and-filter). Two generators run with
   OPPOSING mandates — "bull" (maximize upside) and "lean" (maximize resilience)
   — each reading the diagnose + market files so the options stand on real
   findings. Each proposes 2-3 distinct options with "what must be true". The
   lead then filters: dedupe, drop the dominated ones, score the rest against
   the decision criteria, and keep the 2-3 strongest. (Deep mode adds a
   contrarian generator for wider divergence.)

STEP 5 — Synthesis. The lead picks the recommended option and builds a draft
   using the Pyramid Principle (governing thought -> 3 arguments -> backing),
   naming the load-bearing assumptions explicitly — the panel attacks those.

STEP 6 — Pressure-test (the core). Only NOW does the lead spawn a PANEL of
   verifiers, each with a different lens — assumptions, war-game, execution &
   economics. Their job is NOT to confirm but to REFUTE. The rule that makes it
   robust: a claim that >=2 of the panel independently call fatal does not
   survive — it gets fixed, hedged, or the recommendation changes. (Deep mode
   loops the war-game lens until risks run dry, and runs a tournament to pick
   between options that are still close.)

STEP 7 — Deliverable. The lead folds the surviving critique in and ships a
   board-ready decision memo + narrative: decision question, recommendation,
   options with trade-offs, business case, top risks + mitigations, first 90
   days, and a 60-second story with hostile Q&A. Written to a durable file so
   you can take it into the meeting.

STEP 8 — Cleanup. The lead shuts the teammates down and cleans up the team.


3. WHAT IT'S WORTH
------------------
- An analysis that SURVIVES the meeting. Not just advice, but advice run through
  a panel of hostile verifiers where a weakness two of them agree on doesn't get
  to slide. You know the weak spots before anyone in the room finds them.

- Structure on a vague or large question. A merger or strategy question gets
  split into diagnose / market / competing-options / risk, each with a proven
  method — instead of one lump of gut feel.

- Speed and depth at once. A team of "consultants" works in parallel waves in
  minutes, not a sequential session over hours. And you can steer live.

- A concrete, shareable deliverable: a decision memo you put straight into a
  leadership or board meeting.

- Reusable and portable. It's one self-contained folder you can hand to a
  colleague as-is.

COST / WHEN NOT. A standard run is ~7 live sessions across its waves (more in
deep mode), so noticeably more tokens than an ordinary chat. Use it for real
decisions, not a quick question, and default to standard mode unless the call is
genuinely high-stakes. For a single framework, load that framework directly.
